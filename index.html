03Aster Fraud AnalysisData science & investigationAcademic / ResearchSynthetic expense-fraud analysis. I built a validation and modelling workflow: Random Forest detected 44 of 60 planted cases with 12 false alarms on 800 synthetic test claims.

Aster is a fictional company with generated claims and planted fraud labels. I validate and reconcile records, quarantine invalid entries, and compare rules with logistic regression and Random Forest.

Saved Test Results

On 800 synthetic test claims containing 60 planted fraud cases, Random Forest detected 44 cases with 12 false alarms. Baseline rules detected 24 cases with 35 false alarms.

Chronological splits separate training, validation, and testing. Random Forest was selected using validation F1, not the test results. Association mining and graph analysis provide additional investigative context.

These results describe a single synthetic generator, not real-world fraud detection performance. Alerts are leads for review, not proof of fraud.

04Cloud Vulnerability PrioritisationRisk-based analysisAcademic / ResearchJoint retrospective research on 13,819 network-exploitable CVEs. With Kaung Khant Zaw (Ralph), I compared CVSS, EPSS, and metadata-based models against CISA KEV membership.

Co-authored with Kaung Khant Zaw (Ralph). We joined NVD vulnerability metadata, the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue, and FIRST EPSS scores for 13,819 network-exploitable CVEs published January–June 2024.

Comparison and Limits

We compared standalone CVSS and EPSS with logistic regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest using 11 NVD/CVSS predictors. EPSS and the KEV labels were excluded from the model inputs. EPSS performed best in the saved results, identifying 3 of 10 positive test cases with 2 false alarms.

This is retrospective KEV-membership classification, not a forecast of future exploitation. The EPSS snapshot is dated 30 March 2026; the KEV acquisition date was not recorded. Only 50 records were KEV-listed, with 10 in the test set. Later snapshots, random splitting, training-fitted thresholds, and the small positive population limit generalisation. Network exploitability is a proxy for relevance to cloud-hosted services; the study does not model asset exposure, business criticality, or patch feasibility.
